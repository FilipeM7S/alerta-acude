Prototype. It ranks reservoirs for sampling; it does not replace laboratory testing of drinking water.

How it works

Above 20,000 cyanobacteria cells/mL, Brazil's drinking-water rule (Portaria GM/MS nº 888/2021) requires cyanotoxin tests at least weekly. COGERH samples each reservoir only once per quarter. Alerta Açude uses a satellite image from the last few days to decide which reservoirs to sample first.

  1. 1 · Ground truth

    COGERH lab reports

    28 quarterly reports (Feb/2016 to Feb/2025) parsed into 3,679 lab samples from 156 reservoirs, cross-checked against COGERH's online database (99.6% of counts agree).

  2. 2 · Satellite

    Sentinel-2 water colour

    Only clean water pixels within 1 km of the sampling point. NDCI (chlorophyll), NDTI (turbidity), MNDWI (water signal), open-water share. 614 lab samples matched to a clear image within ±5 days.

  3. 3 · Model

    Logistic regression

    6 inputs: the 4 satellite indices plus stored volume and its change. Trained on 2016–2020 campaigns; the Alert threshold is set on 2020–2021 to catch at least 80% of blooms. Its coefficients are the explanation: NDCI dominates (+1.39).

  4. 4 · Every week

    Priority ranking

    Latest clear image (last 30 days) + today's volume → priority 1 … , an Alert / Lower risk band, the top 3 reasons in plain language, and the last lab result alongside.

Data: COGERH / Portal Hidrológico do Ceará, Copernicus Sentinel-2 L2A (Microsoft Planetary Computer), OpenStreetMap. Code and full method: github.com/FilipeM7S/alerta-acude.

Limits

  • Scores are priorities, not probabilities

    Balanced class weights pull the scores down: on validation and test data, reservoirs scored 0.30 or less were blooms 77% of the time. The 12 clean validation samples are too few to calibrate them. Use the rank and the band.

  • The satellite sees chlorophyll, not cells

    NDCI follows lab chlorophyll-a closely (Spearman 0.81) but cyanobacteria counts less so (0.41). Riacho do Sangue had 191,134 cells/mL with only 12.85 µg/L of chlorophyll-a. Green water from other algae can also raise a false alarm.

  • It under-warns in the dry season

    In Test 2 it missed 22 dry-season blooms, against 10 for the last lab result. This map is for November, so treat "Lower risk" with extra caution.

  • Clouds and geometry

    of reservoirs had no clear image in the last 30 days. The open-water share partly measures the sampling area's shape; affected reservoirs are flagged low confidence.

  • It does not replace lab tests

    It is a prototype that ranks reservoirs for sampling. Only a laboratory can confirm cyanobacteria counts and toxins in drinking water.
